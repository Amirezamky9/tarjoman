Heidegger's fundamental ontology in Being and Time destabilized classical Cartesian epistemology by interrogating the primordial meaning of Being. Central to this phenomenological investigation is the concept of Dasein, whose constitutive structure is characterized as Being-in-the-world.

The ontological difference between Being (Sein) and beings (Seiendes) demands a thorough deconstruction of traditional Western metaphysics. Within existential phenomenology, human existence cannot be conceived as an isolated thinking subject (cogito), but rather as temporal care thrown into historical situatedness.

Through this transcendental hermeneutic, consciousness ceases to be a detached observer; instead, existentialism discloses our authentic possibility of being toward death.
