This empirical study evaluates the hypothesis regarding relativistic mass-energy conservation under extreme gravitational conditions. Following rigorous research methodology, our experimental cohort analyzed the extensive dataset across 120 independent observation cycles.

According to relativistic electrodynamics, the invariant energy relation is formulated as $E=mc^2$, which determines particle acceleration thresholds within the magnetic containment field. As documented in previous peer-reviewed literature (Smith et al., 2024), quantitative measurements revealed a statistically significant correlation (p-value < 0.001) between radiation flux and velocity variance.

Standard deviation across all continuous variables remained within the acceptable confidence interval of 95%. These empirical findings substantiate our primary hypothesis and provide robust confirmation of theoretical predictions.
